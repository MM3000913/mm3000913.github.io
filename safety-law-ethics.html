03 / Use two lenses

Law sets a floor. Ethics asks what is fair.

These ideas overlap, but they are not interchangeable. An ethical concern is not automatically proof of a legal violation.

United States / Federal law

What does the law prohibit?

Section 5 of the Federal Trade Commission Act, 15 U.S.C. § 45, prohibits unfair or deceptive acts or practices in or affecting commerce. It can apply when a business misleads people about data practices or handles data in an unfair way. It does not make every use of GPS illegal; applying it depends on facts and the business involved.

Ethics / Professional responsibility

What should a responsible designer do?

Ask whether precise background collection is necessary, whether people can understand and refuse partner sharing, and how long the data is kept. Data minimization and clear choices are ethical safeguards even when no specific law has been shown to be violated.

Real enforcement / FTC, 2024

The FTC said X-Mode Social and successor Outlogic sold precise location data associated with mobile advertising IDs; the data could reveal visits to sensitive places. The FTC finalized a consent order in April 2024 prohibiting the companies from sharing or selling sensitive location data. That company-specific order is an example of enforcement, not a blanket ban on navigation apps or GPS.

04 / Make it safer

A practical privacy check.

Before granting access, compare what a feature needs with what the app is asking for.

  1. Limit the permission. Prefer access only while using the app when background tracking is not needed.
  2. Check the recipient. Look for whether precise location is shared with analytics or advertising partners.
  3. Revisit the choice. Review permissions and delete apps or access you no longer need.
